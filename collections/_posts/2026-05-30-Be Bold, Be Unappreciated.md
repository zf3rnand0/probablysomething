A mildly coherant rambling on Birmingham and regional identities.

![Street art mural of "Be Bold, Be Birmingham" in Degbeth](/assets/images/blogImgs/290526-BeBoldBeBrum.jpg)

This post was promted by this week's penultimate (😭) episode of [Sonic Spectrum on URY](https://www.mixcloud.com/URY1350/sonic-spectrum-28052026/), where Lenny and Amy played anthems from their home cities (Newcastle and London). Even though I currently live in Worcester (and have for almost half my life), the post will mostly centre around Birmingham, as I understand why Worcester lacks a strong regional identity (hint: it's tiny).

On the episode mentioned above, Amy talks about how when, in her first year of uni, when she was homesick, she heard people from her area singing "The Fog on the Tyne" and it made her feel at home. I tried to think of a similar song or other media for Birmingham - **and I found nothing.** This post is about why that is - how can England's second largest city (which I've personally lived in for just over half my life) leave such a small imprint of reconisable culture on my mind?

Growing up, I've never really thought about what it means to be the resident of a particular place. Having lived in the Midlands my entire life, I've never really experienced the strong love/hate people have for "The North" and "The South" (this is a big thing in England, if you don't understand, see the [North-o-Meter](https://www.tomscott.com/usvsth3m/north-o-meter/)). For a long time, I've been aware of the country's general view of birmingham - unattractive accent, terrible bin collections, brutalist architechture and overall being a "Fucking shithole".

| ![Image of Birmingham bin strikes (2025)](/assets/images/blogImgs/290529-BirminghamRubbish.jpg) |
| *What the average Brit pictures when they hear the word "Birmingham" (An actual BirminghamLive photo from the bin strikes)* |

Now this in of itself isn't the regional identity of a city. Plenty of cities have negative sterotypes, Scousers are criminals, Londoners are rude and Bristolians are uneducated farming simpletons. However, each of these cities have their own identities: Liverpool has The Beatles and two Premier League-winning teams, London is the capital (so has everything), and Bristol is the up-and-coming indie city of England, and has Wallace and Gromit. This isn't to say that Birmingham has no culture - in fact, the city is the home of many cultural phenomenoms - Duran Duran, Black Sabbath and J. R. R. Tolkein to name a few. Birmingham is an unrivaled melting pot of people from all walks of life, background and ethnicities. I have never been in a city quite as multicultural as Birmingham. However, ask somebody about Birmingham, and most likely, they'll quote the [Happiest Man in Birmingham](https://youtu.be/jS94-_zy3Dg?si=cVIwsUn8nWo1o1TY). Even those who live in and love the city often do so with an almost ironic sense of pride, as everyone knows how the rest of the country thinks of them.

To try and offset this negative view of Birmingham on the run up to the Commonwealth Games 2022, the council introduced the slogan "Be Bold, Be Birmingham". If you google the phrase, you'll come across a [case study](https://www.local.gov.uk/case-studies/birmingham-city-council-emboldened-develop-place-narrative), that claims that the campaign has been an amazing success. While it is almost impossible to visit Brum without seeing this slogan plastered on council signs, it hasn't actually done much to change the national or even local image of the area. To be clear, this kind of push needs to be made - local pride is an important concept to inspire people to make their area the best it can be - however, the image of the city hasn't changed much due to the slogan itself.

Despite the bad image, accent, and sense of self-worth, I do think that Birmingham can improve it's regional identity. Firstly, the city itself needs to improve. This is already being done, with many old, brutalist hellscape buildings being ripped out and replaced (think New Street Station and the old library). While this does considerably improve the city's atmosphere, the council needs to be careful to protect historical buildings from the wrecking ball. Buildings such as the Ringway Centre and Fiveways House need to be preserved, in order to showcase an honour the city's rich heritage.

But most of all, the city needs to have something unite us. We need more initiatives similar to the constantly-extended Ozzy Ozbourne exhibit at the museum to show what talent the city truly has. If we have an "anthem" to all know and recognise, then maybe Birmingham has a chance to show the country, and itself what an amazing city it truly is.
